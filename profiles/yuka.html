The Women of Terra Firma

Yuka Hayabusa

Yuka Shadow — Bass Guitar

Razorwire Chainsaw Dolls

Born on November 17, 1994, Yuka Hayabusa's earliest memories are of a happy childhood.

Her parents were neither wealthy nor desperately poor. They lived modestly, loved their daughter, and encouraged her to grow at her own pace. For the first six years of her life, Yuka knew the security of having two parents who cared deeply for her.

Then, one autumn evening, everything changed.

A Nostrava attacked the family home.

Yuka's father, a physically imposing man in his own right, was killed during the assault. Her mother suffered an even more horrifying fate as the creature began consuming her from within. An oil lamp overturned during the struggle, setting the small house ablaze.

Terrified, Yuka hid inside a closet, listening to the destruction of everything she had ever known.

She expected to die.

Instead, the barricaded front door burst open.

The man who entered was FOXALIVE, already becoming known as the Monster Killer during his second year of battling the creatures that plagued Terra Firma.

He tackled the Nostrava to the floor and forced glowing spheres of explosive magic down its throat. Moments later, the creature erupted in a violent explosion, somehow contained by magic that prevented the blast from destroying the burning house around them.

For Yuka, the monster that had destroyed her family was finally gone.

But her parents were still dead.

Clinging to the man who had saved her, the six-year-old asked why they had to die.

FOXALIVE did not offer a comforting lie or a childish explanation. He told her the truth: sometimes life simply wasn't fair.

But he also promised her something.

He had her.

And for that frightened little girl, it was enough.

In the years that followed, Yuka's life took an unexpected turn. Rather than disappearing into an uncertain future of orphanages and unfamiliar guardians, she began receiving anonymous support.

Money, food, housing, and the necessities of everyday life were provided without demands or explanations.

She attended school, grew up in relative stability, and eventually graduated. Although someone continued to watch over her financial well-being, Yuka was left to handle much of her daily life independently.

That independence became one of her defining characteristics.

She learned to rely upon herself, developed a dry and sometimes cutting sense of humor, and became increasingly comfortable with solitude.

Music eventually offered another form of freedom.

Teaching herself bass guitar, Yuka developed an inventive playing style that emphasized creativity and expression. After graduating from high school, she began performing in clubs throughout Cincinnati's west side, where her unusual bass solos and distinctive musicianship attracted attention.

She performed with several bands, including Savior/Destroyer, On the Lam, and Haterade.

Her talent was rarely questioned.

Her interest in socializing with her fellow musicians, however, was another matter entirely.

Yuka generally kept to herself. She played her music, fulfilled her commitments, and saw little reason to become particularly close to the people sharing the stage with her.

That began to change in 2019.

During a visit to a club, Yuka met Miki Shima, Saya Hayashima, and Shizuka Hagimoto.

Something about the three women caught her attention. Their personalities, shared musical interests, and willingness to accept her without demanding that she become someone else gradually drew Yuka out of her self-imposed isolation.

She began spending more time with them, allowing friendships to develop naturally.

The arrival of Kanako Uribo brought another important relationship into her life.

And eventually, Yuka found herself reunited with FOXALIVE.

By then, she had begun investigating the mysterious support she had received throughout her childhood and adolescence. The more she examined the circumstances, the more convinced she became that FOXALIVE had been responsible.

When confronted with her suspicions, he neither confirmed nor denied them.

Yuka drew her own conclusions.

Today, performing under the name Yuka Shadow, she serves as the bassist for Razorwire Chainsaw Dolls.

Intelligent, snarky, observant, and armed with a remarkably dry sense of humor, Yuka remains a fiercely independent woman. Her good heart is often concealed beneath a calm exterior and the world-weariness of someone who learned life's harsher lessons far too young.

But with Kanako, Miki, Saya, Shizuka, April Nanakusa, and FOXALIVE, she has found something she once thought unnecessary.

People whose company she genuinely enjoys.

Yuka doesn't need to be surrounded by others to feel complete.

She simply wants to be with these particular people.

And for Yuka Hayabusa, that makes all the difference.
