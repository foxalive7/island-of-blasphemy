The Women of Terra Firma

Kanako Uribo

The Woman Behind Destroyah

Before she became the commanding voice of Razorwire Chainsaw Dolls, Kanako Uribo was known to wrestling fans as Destroyah, one of the most formidable women ever to step into a World League Wrestling Federation ring.

Growing up in Cincinnati, Ohio, Kanako was already six feet tall by early high school. Her unusual height made her stand out, often attracting the kind of attention she would rather have avoided. Professional wrestling offered something different. Inside the ring, the very thing that made her feel out of place became one of her greatest strengths.

She began her career on Cincinnati's independent wrestling circuit, performing under names such as The Amazing Giantess and Terra Destroyer. Her imposing stature and hard-nosed wrestling style eventually attracted the attention of the World League Wrestling Federation, a dramatic leap in production, spectacle, and opportunity.

At WLWF Royale Shutdown 2008, Kanako debuted as Destroyah. With shorter hair, elaborate face paint, and a distinctive blue bodysuit, she presented an intimidating figure whose natural beauty was impossible to conceal. She quickly established herself as a dominant competitor and became a multiple-time WLWF World Women's Champion.

Yet behind Destroyah's formidable appearance was an easygoing young woman who initially struggled with shyness.

That began to change through her friendship with FOXALIVE, then the WLWF's booker. His encouragement helped Kanako find confidence beyond her wrestling persona. He pushed her to improve, but also encouraged her to respect her own limits, prioritize her well-being, and make decisions for herself.

As Kanako became more comfortable expressing her personality, Destroyah grew into one of the promotion's most popular performers.

When the WLWF closed in 2018, Kanako found herself facing an uncertain future.

She eventually took a job as a bouncer at a strip club. Music had always been important to her, but an unexpected equipment failure revealed just how important it might become.

When the club's sound system malfunctioned, Kanako stepped in and began singing. The enthusiastic reaction from the audience surprised her—and suggested that she might have another career waiting.

Over the following years, Kanako formed close friendships with fellow musicians Miki Shima, Saya Hayashima, Yuka Hayabusa, and Shizuka Hagimoto. What began as evenings spent drinking, smoking, playing music, and enjoying one another's company gradually developed into something much bigger.

In late 2025, the five women emerged as Razorwire Chainsaw Dolls, releasing their debut single, Wrecking Ball, a song reflecting the events of that year.

Their first full-length album, Crushing Hammer of Justice, followed on July 15, 2026.

Only this time, she doesn't need face paint or a wrestling persona to captivate an audience.

She's simply Kanako Uribo.

And that's more than enough.
